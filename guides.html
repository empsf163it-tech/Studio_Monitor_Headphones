LESS JARGON. MORE CLARITY.

KNOW YOUR SOUND.

A friendly introduction to the features that matter when choosing studio monitor headphones.

01

DESIGN

Closed-back vs. open-back

Closed-back: Usually keeps more sound in and helps reduce sound leaking into a microphone. Often useful for recording.

Open-back: Lets air and sound pass through the earcups. Often chosen for listening and mixing in a quiet room, but leaks sound and provides little isolation.

02

CONNECTION

Wired or wireless?

Wired headphones are a common studio choice because they do not need charging and avoid Bluetooth latency. Wireless can be convenient for casual listening, but check latency and connection needs before using them for recording.

03

COMFORT

Comfort is a feature

Consider weight, clamping force, ear-pad material, and heat. If possible, try headphones on before buying—especially if you work in long sessions.

04

COMPATIBILITY

What about impedance?

Impedance is an electrical property that affects how a headphone works with a source. Higher-impedance models may need more voltage to reach a useful listening level. Check your audio interface or headphone amp specs.

05

DURABILITY

Maintenance & spare parts

Extend the lifespan of your studio headphones by selecting models with detachable cables and easily replaceable ear cushions. Storing them on a dedicated desktop stand helps maintain headband clamp tension.

06

BEFORE YOU BUY

Use this quick checklist

  • What will I mainly do: record, mix, podcast, or listen?
  • Do I need sound isolation to avoid microphone bleed?
  • Will my computer, phone, or audio interface drive them properly?
  • Are they comfortable enough for my typical session length?
  • Have I checked the exact model, warranty, and current price?

TECHNICAL QUICK REFERENCE

Impedance & source matching

16Ω – 48Ω

Mobile & Laptop Direct

Low-impedance models run efficiently off smartphones, laptops, and USB dongles without needing extra amplification.

80Ω – 150Ω

Studio Interface Standard

Ideal balance for USB audio interfaces (Focusrite, Universal Audio, MOTU) providing plenty of volume and dynamic range.

250Ω – 600Ω

Dedicated Headphone Amps

High-impedance models designed for high-voltage studio consoles and specialized headphone amps for minimal distortion.

LONG-TERM WEARING

Hearing safety & session breaks

The 60 / 60 Rule

Listen at no more than 60% maximum volume for a maximum of 60 minutes at a time to protect your hearing acuity for mixing.

Ear Fatigue Recovery

Take 10-minute quiet breaks every hour. Prolonged high SPL monitoring causes temporary threshold shifts that compromise balance decisions.

AUDIO SPECTRUM MAP

Frequency response bands

Sub-Bass & Bass Range 20Hz – 250Hz

Controls kick drum punch, bass guitar depth, and low-end synth translation.

Midrange Core 250Hz – 4,000Hz

Where lead vocal presence, snare snaps, and acoustic guitars sit in the mix.

Treble & Air 4,000Hz – 20,000Hz

Captures cymbal shimmer, vocal air, and delicate reverb spatial decay.

01

Flat Frequency Tuning

Unlike consumer headphones that boost bass by +6dB, studio monitors keep all bands balanced.

02

Equal Loudness Listening

Monitor at moderate volumes (70-80dB SPL) so low and high frequencies remain in natural acoustic balance.

03

Reference Calibration

Listen to professionally mastered tracks on your studio headphones to build muscle memory for accurate mixing.
